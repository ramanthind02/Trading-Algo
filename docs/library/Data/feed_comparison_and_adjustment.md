# Feed fidelity & reference-series quality — the evidence

> The evidence behind [[feed_and_execution_decision]]: how the Darwinex (MT5) CFD
> feed compares to Norgate futures/cash/ETF on **daily** data, why some Norgate
> reference series are unsafe as return references, and the gold/silver
> future↔CFD transfer study. The *adjustment architecture* (additive/ratio/
> unadjusted) lives in [[futures_research_data]]; the timezone mechanics in
> [[mt5_timezones]]. Reproduce with `research/feed_comparison/feed_*`, `research/feed_comparison/gs_*`,
> `research/feed_comparison/norgate_reference_lag_scan.py`.

## 1. The CFD feed is faithful (daily)

After fixing the [[mt5_timezones]] offset (snapshot the last M1 close at **broker
23:00 = 16:00 ET**):

| Darwinex CFD/ETF ↔ true Norgate | daily corr | ann. tracking err |
|---|---|---|
| SP500 (index CFD) ↔ SPX cash | 0.988 | 3.0% |
| SPY (ETF CFD) ↔ SPY ETF | 0.995 | 1.9% |
| QQQ (ETF CFD) ↔ NDX cash | 0.997 | 1.9% |
| XAUUSD ↔ GLD | 0.996 | — |
| XAGUSD ↔ SLV | 0.995 | — |

On a same-day basis the **CFD/ETF tracks the cash index *better* than the
back-adjusted future does** (SPY-CFD TE 1.9% vs ES-future TE 4.1% vs SPX). The
future's only edge is **history length**, not fidelity — which is exactly why
futures are kept for index research and nothing else.

## 2. Signals transfer futures → CFD (portfolio substitution)

Positions generated by the prop portfolio (ES/NQ/GC, `equal_signal`, τ=0.20, frozen
vault fits) on **futures**, OOS 2023-01→2026-05, P&L recomputed under each feed:

| Execution feed | Ann ret | Vol | Sharpe | TE vs real-fut | corr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Futures additive | 14.27% | 10.4% | 1.37 | 1.48% | 0.993 |
| Futures real (unadj) | 18.78% | 11.2% | **1.68** | — | 1.00 |
| CFD (SP500/QQQ/XAUUSD) | 19.23% | 11.2% | 1.72 | 2.14% | 0.982 |
| ETF (SPY/QQQ/GLD) | 19.48% | 11.2% | 1.74 | 2.30% | 0.979 |

→ **Signals built on futures execute on CFD/ETF with Sharpe preserved** (~2%/yr TE).
→ The additive-vs-real gap (1.37 vs 1.68) re-confirms: **never compute returns from
the additive series** (see [[futures_research_data]] §2).

## 3. Gold/silver: future and CFD are the same instrument

Clock-aligned daily-return correlations (2018+ overlap; ET = stored − 7h):

| Pair | CFD mark (stored / ET) | corr |
|---|---|---|
| XAUUSD ↔ **GC future** | 20:00 / ~13:00 ET (gold settle) | **0.969** |
| XAUUSD ↔ GLD | 23:00 / 16:00 ET (cash close) | 0.996 |
| XAGUSD ↔ **SI future** | 20:00 / ~13:00 ET | **0.966** |
| XAGUSD ↔ SLV | 23:00 / 16:00 ET | 0.995 |

The CFD *is* spot gold/silver: 0.97 to the future, 0.99+ to the ETF — the residual is
basis/financing + settlement timing, not a feed defect. **CFD history is clean and
deep** (XAUUSD 28.1 yr / 7,433 days, XAGUSD 23.7 yr / 6,289 days; 0 gaps >4d, 0 bad
prints), so gold/silver research is **CFD-native** — the futures bring nothing here.

**Per-sleeve transfer** (production nodes streamed over each feed; CFD marked at the
settle clock):

| sleeve | window | agree% | Sharpe CFD | Sharpe FUT_real |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GC robust-trend-breakout | 2023–26 | 97.6% | 1.10 | 1.08 |
| GC robust-trend-breakout | 2004–26 | 96.6% | 0.63 | 0.64 |
| SI sma-regime-trend | 2004–26 | 96.2% | 0.26 | 0.15 |
| **SI donchian-MR** | 2004–26 | 91.8% | 0.37 | 0.35 |

Trend/breakout sleeves transfer almost perfectly. The **silver mean-reversion**
sleeve (tight time-exit) is the most feed-sensitive at ~89–92% agreement and can
sign-flip its short-window Sharpe — it's the one to watch on the CFD-native
promotion gate (and it's weak on *every* feed regardless).

## 4. Reference-series quality — clock-time & adjustment pathologies

A daily bar is a **snapshot at one instant per day**. Two series only give a
meaningful same-day return if **both snapshots share a clock-time** *and* both are on
a **multiplicative** price scale. Two Norgate pathologies break this:

### (a) Commodity-cash series are mis-timed (LBMA/London fix)

`cash_commodities` GC/SI/WTI/PL/PA/CU/HHNG are a **morning London benchmark** (LBMA
gold PM fix 15:00 London = 10:00 ET; silver fix 12:00 London = 07:00 ET; LME copper),
**not** a 16:00-ET close. Best-lag daily-return corr to the matching future/ETF is
**0.26–0.86** (silver even peaks at **lag +1**) — vs 0.99 for equity cash. The
*prices* are correct (GC cash = real spot); only the **time-of-day** is wrong.

| family | use as same-day return reference | do **not** use |
|---|---|---|
| Equity index | cash / future / ETF (all 16:00 ET) | — |
| **Precious/base metal** | **ETF (GLD/SLV/…) or future settle** | the cash fix (GC/SI/PL/PA/CU) |
| **Energy** | **ETF (USO/UNG) or future settle** | the cash "spot" (WTI/HHNG) |
| FX | FX future (preferred) or spot | — |
| Continuous future, %-math | **ratio** series | the **additive** `D_{T}` |

The **control that proves it's timing, not units**: equity cash (SPX/NDX/DJI/RUT) is
clean at lag-0 corr 0.99 because cash, future, and ETF all close 16:00 ET; FX spot is
mostly clean (0.93–0.965). Only the commodity rows — which snapshot in London — are
off. This is the **same root cause** as the [[mt5_timezones]] broker-offset bug.

### (b) Additive back-adjustment

The continuous-futures additive convention distorts %-returns and can go negative;
the **ratio** series is the fix. Full treatment in [[futures_research_data]] §2–3.

## 5. The permanent guardrail

`data_platform/data_quality/reference_series.py` is a read-only validator (exits
non-zero = number of hard failures) for CI / scheduled runs:

1. **Alignment** — every declared same-day return reference must have best-lag corr
   ≥ 0.95 **at lag 0** vs its 16:00-ET partner (non-zero best lag / lag-0 < 0.95 FAILS).
2. **No negative prices** on any series tagged for %-returns.
3. **Adjustment sanity** — classify each future's `D` vs `D_unadj` as additive vs
   ratio; flag additive used for %-math (WARN; FAIL under `--strict-additive`).

It is wired into the portfolio cache preflight via the scoped
`validate_consumed_series(config.tickers)` (loud WARN by default; hard FAIL only on a
genuinely broken *consumed* series — CL's real WTI Apr-2020 negative is a WARN).

```powershell
.\venv\Scripts\python.exe -m data_platform.data_quality.reference_series
```

## 6. ETF vs CFD — which to trade

| | CFD (index/commodity) | ETF / ETF-CFD |
|---|---|---|
| Leverage | built-in | needs margin borrow |
| Shorting | symmetric, trivial | locate/borrow (ETF) |
| Session | ~23h | 6.5h (09:30–16:00 ET) only |
| Carry | daily swap @ 17:00 ET (long pays; Fri ×3) | expense ratio (SPY .09 / QQQ .20 / GLD .40%) |
| Calendar | ~56–60 extra thin US-holiday sessions | exact NYSE calendar |
| Coverage | ES→SP500, GC→XAUUSD, CL→XTIUSD, YM→WS30; **no Nasdaq index CFD** | SPY, QQQ, GLD, SLV, TLT, DIA |

**Trade CFDs by default** (leverage + symmetric shorting + ~23h, one Darwinex
terminal); use **ETF-CFDs only where no index CFD exists** (Nasdaq→QQQ, rates→TLT).
